As the role of a chief financial officer continues to evolve, it becomes increasingly important for CFOs to use business intelligence (BI) as a strategic tool. By understanding and analyzing company data, CFOs can provide valuable insights that help expand their influence throughout the organization.

In order to continue building strong partnerships with other business executives, CFOs must become experts at translating data into insightful analysis and actionable advice. By doing so, CFOs can help make informed decisions about important issues such as marketing campaigns, supply chain adjustments, and customer service. With the use of BI, CFOs can play a vital role in the success of their organization.

# What is Business Intelligence (BI)?

[Business intelligence (BI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_intelligence) is a technology-driven process that helps organizations collect, store, and analyze data to better understand their business operations. By using BI, CFOs can gain insights into key areas of the business such as marketing, finance, and operations. With this information, CFOs can make more informed decisions about how to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of their organization.

To continue developing relationships throughout the organization, [CFOs must become experts](/blog/what-ceos-want-from-their-cfos) at turning a wide range of data into useful analysis and practical recommendations. Is that new marketing campaign living up to its expectations? Are supply chain changes paying off in improved on-time delivery of parts and raw materials? Did changing our call center personnel to work from home improve or decrease customer service?

That’s where business intelligence systems and dashboards come in. BI technology aids finance executives in collecting data from both internal and external sources and producing analyses that monitor relevant information and create key performance indicators (KPIs) that are important.

## Why Is Business Intelligence Important to CFOs?

A CFO understands the financial health of an organization is dependent on accurate, up-to-date data. A BI strategy can assist your company remains competitive and financially stable by providing rapid access to comprehensive cash flow insights, expense management optimization possibilities, and the capacity to make educated cost and profitability decisions, all of which can be linked back to business success.

Introducing BI into the workplace does more than just automate processes–it also makes your finance team’s job easier. With quick and reliable access to data-driven insights, they can work more efficiently and make better decisions about what will help the company grow. In addition, by promoting a data-driven culture, they become trusted advisors for other departments in the company.

## What are The Benefits of Using Business Intelligence (BI) for CFOs?

There are many benefits of using BI for CFOs, some of which include:

*   Improved decision making: With BI, CFOs can make better-informed decisions about important issues such as marketing campaigns, supply chain adjustments, and customer service.
*   Increased efficiency: BI can help CFOs work more efficiently by providing quick and reliable access to data-driven insights.
*   Enhanced collaborative decision-making: By promoting a data-driven culture, CFOs can become trusted advisors for other departments in the company. This can lead to enhanced collaboration when making decisions that affect the entire organization.

## How Finance Uses Business Intelligence (BI)

Finance executives use business intelligence in a number of ways, some of which include:

### Monitoring financial performance

BI can help finance executives monitor key financial metrics such as revenue, expenses, and profitability. This information can be used to make informed decisions about how to improve the financial health of the organization.

### Expense reporting and management

The finance department can use dashboards and analyses to obtain a thorough understanding of employee spending, uphold expense policies, and monitor T&E trends. Tactics that work well include arranging for dashboards and reports to be sent via email to help managers keep track of their staff, setting up alerts and notifications to efficiently manage to spend, and connecting BI tools organization’s expenses, invoices, and online travel booking systems.

### Planning for future growth

BI can also be used to help finance executives plan for future growth. By analyzing historical data, they can identify trends and patterns that can be used to forecast future financial performance.

### Risk management

Leaders need to swiftly respond when crises crop up, and that’s where BI and data management tools come in handy. Financial performance analytics give the finance department an overview of the organization’s credit and market risk.

And because KPIs are placed prominently on any dashboard with BI software, your team can always be aware of what takes precedence at a glance. Furthermore, should investment opportunities develop or regulators request more frequent submissions, BI makes it easier to generate risk assessments accordingly.

### Profitability management:

Pricing anomalies can be discovered by analyzing data from thousands of sales transactions, each record containing detailed characteristics of your products and customers. B2B systems can evaluate variables such as channel profitability, discount impact, lifetime revenue contributions for different groups, and more using these insights. CFOs and other business executives can concentrate on acquiring and retaining profitable clients by using this data.

#### The Bottom Line

Business intelligence is a powerful tool that can be used by CFOs to improve decision-making, increase efficiency, and enhance collaborative decision-making. Additionally, BI can be used by finance departments to monitor financial performance, expenses, and risk. When used correctly, BI can be a valuable asset for organizations of all sizes.
